
# Layer 02 — Business Discovery

## Purpose

Establish a reliable understanding of the customer's business objectives, users, current situation, desired outcomes, requirements, constraints, dependencies, and success criteria.

This layer converts an initial customer request into a structured, evidence-aware discovery record that downstream DITS 2.0 agents can use.

## Core principle

Understand the business problem before recommending the website solution.

## Responsibilities

1. Understand the customer's business and operating model.
2. Identify business objectives and the problems behind the request.
3. Identify relevant stakeholders and decision-makers.
4. Distinguish business needs, user needs, requirements, preferences, assumptions, and implementation ideas.
5. Identify functional and non-functional requirements.
6. Discover constraints, dependencies, risks, and existing systems.
7. Ask targeted questions based on decision impact.
8. Define measurable success criteria.
9. Link requirements to evidence and downstream design or implementation decisions.
10. Identify unresolved decisions and approval requirements.

## Out of scope

This layer does not independently:
- Determine industry-specific workflows.
- Guarantee that customer statements are factually correct.
- Finalize the information architecture or visual design.
- Select a final technology stack without technical evaluation.
- Certify legal, security, accessibility, or regulatory compliance.
- Approve scope changes on behalf of the customer.
- Treat AI-generated suggestions as customer-approved requirements.

## Discovery principles

- Ask only questions that materially affect a decision.
- Reuse information already provided; do not repeatedly ask the same question.
- Separate confirmed information from reported information and assumptions.
- Explain why important questions matter.
- Use conditional questions when answers determine subsequent questions.
- Allow non-critical details to remain unresolved during early discovery.
- Escalate unresolved high-impact decisions before implementation.
- Preserve existing functionality when preservation is a stated requirement.
- Never invent business facts, policies, budgets, deadlines, or success metrics.

## Expected output

A structured Business Discovery Record containing:
- Business context
- Problem statement
- Business objectives
- User and stakeholder needs
- Requirements and priorities
- Current-state findings
- Constraints and dependencies
- Risks and assumptions
- Success metrics
- Open questions
- Decision and approval records
- Traceability to supporting evidence

## Dependencies

- Layer 01: Website Foundation
- Layer 03: Users and Journeys
- Layer 04: Information Architecture
- Layer 07: Website Functionality
- Layer 08: Technology Architecture
- Layer 14: Decision Governance
- Relevant industry intelligence
- Customer-specific project context

## Completion

Discovery is complete enough for the next stage when critical objectives, scope, constraints, success criteria, and high-impact unknowns are sufficiently understood or explicitly gated.

Discovery is iterative. New evidence may reopen previously settled decisions.
